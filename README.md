# 🤖 Enterprise AI Governance & Adoption

## 📌 Project Overview

This project focused on developing and rolling out an enterprise AI governance framework to support responsible and scalable AI adoption across the organization.

I worked with business teams, AI product owners, technology stakeholders, Enterprise Architecture, Integrated Risk Management and other subject matter experts to turn regulatory and internal requirements into practical governance processes.

My role combined AI governance, AI risk management, project management and change management. I was also actively involved in several parts of the implementation, including the AI registry, AI risk assessment methodology, governance processes and training materials.

---

## 🎯 Business Challenge

AI adoption was growing rapidly across different business areas, while teams had different levels of experience and followed different approaches when developing, procuring or using AI solutions.

At the same time, the EU AI Act introduced new regulatory requirements for AI systems depending on their use and risk classification, including requirements around risk management, transparency, human oversight, documentation and accountability.

The organization therefore needed a common governance framework that supported AI adoption while establishing clear expectations for Responsible AI, risk management, ownership and regulatory compliance.

The challenge was to create an approach that was robust enough for enterprise use, aligned with the EU AI Act and practical enough for business teams to apply.

Key topics included:

- Responsible AI principles
- EU AI Act requirements and AI risk classification
- AI risk assessment and governance requirements
- AI system registration and lifecycle management
- Acceptable AI use
- Human oversight and accountability
- Training and organizational adoption

## 🏗️ AI Governance Framework

The framework was designed around the lifecycle of an AI system rather than treating governance as a one-time approval step.

```text
AI Idea / Use Case
        │
        ▼
Initial Assessment
        │
        ▼
AI Registry
        │
        ▼
Risk Classification
        │
        ▼
AI Risk Assessment
        │
        ▼
Governance Requirements
        │
        ▼
Review & Approval
        │
        ▼
Implementation / Use
        │
        ▼
Ongoing Oversight
        │
        ▼
Offboarding
```

Together with the relevant stakeholders, we defined how AI systems should move through this lifecycle and which governance requirements applied depending on the characteristics, use and risk profile of the system.

The objective was to create a consistent enterprise process without making lower-risk AI use unnecessarily difficult.

---

## ⚠️ AI Risk Management

AI risk management was one of the core elements of the framework.

The assessment approach considered risks across areas such as:

- Data quality, privacy and confidentiality
- Accuracy, robustness and reliability
- Bias, fairness and discrimination
- Transparency and explainability
- Human oversight and accountability

I worked with Integrated Risk Management and other SMEs on the structure of the assessment and contributed to the scoring methodology and calculation logic used to evaluate AI risks.

This included discussing how different risk factors should be weighted, combined and translated into consistent assessment results and governance requirements.

The goal was to make AI risk assessment systematic enough for enterprise-wide use while keeping it understandable for business teams.

---

## 📊 AI Registry & Lifecycle Management

A central part of the governance model was establishing transparency over AI systems across the organization.

I worked hands-on on the processes for registering and maintaining AI systems throughout their lifecycle, supported by tools such as LeanIX and ServiceNow.

This included:

- Onboarding new AI systems and use cases
- Capturing ownership and relevant system information
- Connecting systems with risk and governance assessments
- Updating information when systems or use cases changed
- Offboarding AI systems that were no longer in use

The registry provided a central view of AI systems and supported consistent ownership, governance and lifecycle management.

---

## 🧭 Responsible AI & Acceptable Use

Responsible AI principles were translated into practical expectations for employees, AI product owners and technology teams.

This included contributing to an Acceptable AI Use Policy defining how AI tools and solutions could be used within the organization.

Key topics included:

- Appropriate use of enterprise and external AI tools
- Handling of confidential and sensitive information
- Human accountability for AI-supported decisions
- Responsible use of AI-generated content
- Escalation of potentially inappropriate or higher-risk use cases

The objective was to provide clear guardrails while still enabling employees to experiment with and adopt AI responsibly.

---

## 🎓 Training & Change Management

The governance framework could only work if employees understood how to apply it.

I therefore worked hands-on on the development of training materials and supported AI enablement programs reaching more than 1,000 employees.

The training covered topics such as:

- AI governance and Responsible AI
- AI risks and risk assessment
- Acceptable AI use
- AI lifecycle and governance processes
- Responsibilities of business teams and AI product owners

I also supported workshops and a global AI working group connecting AI product owners and stakeholders across the organization to share experiences, align approaches and improve adoption.

Change management was therefore not a separate activity at the end of the project, but part of the rollout from the beginning.
